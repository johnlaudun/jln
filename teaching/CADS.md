## Corpus-Assisted Discourse Studies

Corpus-assisted discourse studies (CADS) differ from corpus linguistics in its focus on the discourse type(s) in a particular corpus. It typically allows for greater freedom in analytical approaches, with researchers typically engaging their corpus in a variety of ways. _Their_ is used intentionally here: CADS can be characterized by the compilation of ad hoc, focused corpora, since very frequently there exists no previously available collection of the discourse type in question. CADS combines methods of text analysis commonly perceived as qualitative and quantitative. CADS describes a great deal of activity across a number of domains, including folkloristics, anthropology, and sociology. In many instances, that work has developed out of extant quantitative methodologies-e.g., the tale-type and motif indices in folklore studies-or has developed in relationship to computational approaches. (Note: Flowerdew \[2012\] and Ancarno \[2020\] are overviews of CADS/CADA published a decade apart.)
